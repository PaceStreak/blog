A habit tracker with a "perfect week" achievement is telling you something
about what it actually values, whether it means to or not: that seven for
seven is the real goal, and everything under that is a lesser version of
succeeding. PaceStreak doesn't have that badge, and it isn't an oversight.

## What a target is for

Every training habit and every non-training habit has a weekly target you
set yourself — three days, five days, whatever the thing can actually take.
The week counts as kept if you meet that target, not if you fill every cell.
Someone training three days a week and someone training six are both either
keeping their streak or not; neither one is behind the other.

A perfect-week badge quietly overrides that. It says the real finish line is
seven regardless of what you asked for, which makes every target below seven
look like a consolation prize you chose out of weakness rather than
judgment. The badge would be lying about the thing the target was built to
say honestly.

## What perfection actually teaches

The failure mode of daily-perfect tracking is well known even without a
citation: once the streak is broken, the cost of a missed day and the cost
of missing every day after it looks the same, so there's no reason to stop
at one. A system that only rewards the unbroken run gives you nothing to
protect once it's already broken.

A weekly target doesn't have that cliff. Missing Tuesday doesn't touch
Wednesday. The week is still recoverable up until it isn't, and even then
there's a freeze and a monthly repair standing between one bad week and the
whole streak resetting. Perfection was never the thing being measured, so
there's nothing catastrophic about falling short of it.

## What does get a badge

Attendance, consistency over months, a target raised and kept — the things
that are actually hard and actually meaningful. None of them require that a
single week look spotless to count. That's the whole point of counting
weeks instead of days: the target is yours, and meeting it is the win,
whatever it looks like from a distance.
