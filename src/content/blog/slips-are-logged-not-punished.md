Most habit trackers are built for habits you want more of: run, read,
meditate. PaceStreak also tracks habits people are trying to break, like
smoking, drinking or late-night scrolling. Those need different defaults.

## Zero is the good number

For a habit you are building, the number goes up: minutes, pages, steps. For a
habit you are breaking, you log **slips**, and a day with zero slips is a
clean day. The logging sheet says so in one line under the counter:

> Zero is a clean day. A slip is logged, not punished.

## Why "not punished"

Someone cutting down on cigarettes who has one after a hard day faces a
choice: log it honestly or leave it out. If logging it wipes out a long run
of progress, the app has taught them to lie to it, and a tracker you lie to
is useless.

So a slip is data, not a verdict. The weekly target decides whether the week
counts, and the note field under the counter asks a practical question
instead of a moral one:

> What was going on? Spotting triggers helps.

## Nobody else ever sees it

A habit's name, amounts and slips never reach a feed, a profile, a group or a
leaderboard. Badges are named generically. Leaderboards may rank the
whole-life streak (did you show up this week?), never which habit or how
much. Someone breaking a drinking habit must never be outed by the product
they are using to do it.

The logging sheet's note field says the same thing in five words, right where
it matters: "Private, like the habit itself."
