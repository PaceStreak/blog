Rest is part of training, not a gap in it. Any program worth following has
days where the plan is to do nothing, on purpose, because that's when the
adaptation from the other days actually happens. A daily streak can't tell
the difference between that and just not showing up.

## The problem with counting every day

If the unit is the day, a rest day and a missed day look exactly the same:
both are a blank cell. The only way to keep the streak whole is to fill
every cell, which means either training through the days you shouldn't, or
watching the number reset on the days you're supposed to be resting. Neither
outcome is one a training plan would recommend.

## What counting the week fixes

PaceStreak's target is weekly — three days, four days, whatever the habit
can actually take — and the week counts as kept once the target is met, not
once every day is filled. Someone whose plan is three training days and four
rest days meets their target on day three and spends the rest of the week
exactly as planned, with nothing left to protect and nothing at risk.

The target itself is the tool for saying how much rest belongs in the week.
Set it at three and you've told the system four days off is the plan, not
an exception you're hoping isn't noticed.

## What this doesn't mean

It doesn't mean rest is invisible. A day marked as planned rest is different
from a day that's simply empty, and the grid can tell them apart — the point
isn't to hide the difference, it's to stop the difference from costing
anything. Rest that was the plan should read as the plan, not as a hole in
an otherwise perfect week that never needed to exist.
