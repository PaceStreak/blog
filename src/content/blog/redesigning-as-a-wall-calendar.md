PaceStreak started as a training tracker and grew into something broader:
reading, water, a language, sleep, a habit you're breaking. The look never
followed. Near-black surfaces, a single lime accent, soft glowing cards. It
read as *gym app*, and it read as every gym app.

So the whole thing was redesigned, app, site and blog together, with one rule:
nothing visual had to survive. Only the product's truth did.

## What the product actually is

Strip the features away and PaceStreak is one gesture: you did the thing today,
so you mark it. Keep marking and a chain forms. The oldest version of that is a
paper calendar on a wall and a red marker. "Don't break the chain" is a
calendar trick before it's an app idea.

That became the world:

- **Paper, not a panel.** A bright coated-paper ground (`#fbfbf8`, deliberately
  not cream), black print, and 1px ruled lines instead of floating cards.
- **Calendar red** for today, for the main action and for the chain. One red,
  used where a printed calendar uses it.
- **A felt-tip set** for habits: learning in blue, health in green, mind in
  violet. Colour is a second cue only; done or not is always the mark itself.
- **Square corners** and no drop shadows. Paper doesn't hover.
- **Condensed numerals** from Archivo, the way dates are printed on a planner.
- **Dark mode** is the same calendar under a desk lamp, not a different product.

## The one moment

Every design needs one thing people remember. Here it's the mark: tap a day and
a marker X draws itself across it in two strokes, in the habit's colour. A
part-done day gets a single stroke, a slip on a habit you're breaking gets a
ring. There's no second checkmark style anywhere in the app, not in checklists
and not in quests, so "done" looks one way.

## What Today became

The home screen used to be a stack of cards. Now it opens on a tear-off page
for today's date next to the whole-life chain in red, and underneath it this
week as a calendar board: one row per habit, seven boxes, today's column ringed.
You can see the whole week and fix yesterday without opening anything.

## What it cost

A redesign that only changes the palette is a reskin, and the first pass was
exactly that: an independent review said it "reads as the old dashboard with
new colours". The rebuild went further: stat cards became ruled rows, pills
became index tabs, the Log button became a marker cap, and the site's features
became one printed planner week.
