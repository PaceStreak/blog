Body weight is one of the easiest numbers to build a reward system around,
and one of the worst. A lower number isn't always a better outcome, a
higher number isn't always progress or its opposite, and a system that
scores it either way is making a claim about someone's body that isn't its
place to make. PaceStreak logs the number and stops there.

## What it's for

Body data — weight, waist, the full tape measure from neck to calf — exists
so you can see a trend over time if you choose to record one. That's the
entire feature. It doesn't feed the streak, it doesn't move XP, it doesn't
appear on a leaderboard next to anyone else's, and there's no badge shaped
around hitting a number. It sits next to your training log because it's
useful to have it in one place, not because the two are supposed to move
together.

## Why the streak can't see it

The streak and XP are earned by the same measure regardless of who's
earning them: showing up against a weekly target. Tying either one to body
weight would mean two people doing identical weeks of work earn different
outcomes depending on a number that training doesn't reliably move in any
particular direction on any particular week. That's already the reasoning
behind XP being blind to how much weight someone's lifting — the same
argument applies even more directly to their own body weight, because unlike
a lift, it's not even meant to only go up.

## Kept private for the same reason habits are

Nothing about body data reaches a feed, a profile or a group, for the same
reason a habit's name doesn't: it's exactly the kind of number that turns a
tracker into a place people perform for an audience instead of a place they
keep a private record. A trend line you can look back on is useful. A trend
line other people can see is a different product, and not one this is
trying to be.
